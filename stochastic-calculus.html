Chapter 1 · No-arbitrage pricing in discrete time

Pricing and replicating a European call

The stock moves up by a factor u or down by d each period, and cash earns a simple rate r per step. Backward induction under the risk-neutral probability gives the call's fair value at every node. A self-financing portfolio that holds Δ shares and starts with X₀ then tracks the call. It replicates the payoff exactly when X₀ equals the fair price V₀; any surplus or deficit just grows in the bank.

The four formulas behind the page

Risk-neutral probability

q = (1 + r − d) / (u − d)

The probability under which the discounted stock is a martingale. No arbitrage is exactly the condition 0 < q < 1.

Backward induction

Vt = [q Vup + (1−q) Vdown] / (1+r)

Start from the payoff max(SN − K, 0) and step back one period at a time.

Delta hedge

Δt = (Vup − Vdown) / (Sup − Sdown)

For a call 0 ≤ Δ ≤ 1: the hedge is long the stock and borrows cash.

Wealth of the hedge

Xt = Vt + (X₀ − V₀)(1+r)t

Holding the call's Δ with the wrong starting capital leaves the gap in the bank, where it earns interest.
