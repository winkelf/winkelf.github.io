Charge in Motion

Six circuits, from a battery and a resistor to an AC loop with a resistor, an inductor and a capacitor. Every dot is a charge carrier. Watch how far and how fast they move, and compare it with the voltage and current on the scope below.

Component values are chosen so the motion is slow enough to follow: AC frequencies are fractions of a hertz and capacitors are in farads. A real 50 Hz circuit does the same things a hundred times faster. Dot speed is proportional to the current, with a scale set per circuit so each one stays readable. In a real copper wire carrying 1 A, electrons drift at roughly a tenth of a millimetre per second, while the push that starts them moving travels along the wire close to the speed of light.
