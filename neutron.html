Nucleons in orbits

Each nucleon moves in the average potential made by all the others, much like electrons in an atom. With a strong spin–orbit force added, the energy levels bunch into shells. A nucleus with a full shell is especially stable. The nucleon counts that fill a shell are the magic numbers: 2, 8, 20, 28, 50, 82 and 126.

Where the magic numbers come from

Build up the potential in three steps, from left to right.

  • Harmonic oscillator. Levels are equally spaced, and shells close at 2, 8, 20, 40, 70, 112 and 168. Only the first three match experiment.
  • Flatter bottom. A real nucleus has roughly constant density, so its potential is flat inside and drops at the edge (Woods–Saxon). Orbits with high l spend more time where the well is deep, so they move down.
  • Spin–orbit. A strong −Vls(r) l·s term splits each level in two. The j = l + ½ partner goes down and j = l − ½ goes up, with a splitting that grows like 2l + 1.

From 1f7/2 upward, the lowered partner of the highest-l orbit (highlighted) drops into the shell below. These intruder levels move the closures to 28, 50, 82 and 126.

Evidence for the magic numbers: the energy needed to remove one nucleon drops sharply just past a magic number. Magic nuclei have unusually high first excited states. Tin (Z = 50) has ten stable isotopes, more than any other element.

Why they behave so differently

α

Alpha: heavy and doubly chargedA helium nucleus, 7300 times heavier than an electron. It loses energy continuously by ionising atoms (Bethe–Bloch) and barely deflects, so every alpha of the same energy stops at almost the same depth: a sharp range. Paper or the dead layer of skin is enough.

β

Beta: light electronsElectrons deflect at almost every collision, so their paths zig-zag and they stop over a spread of depths. Beta decay also gives a spectrum of energies. In high-Z materials such as lead, braking electrons radiate X-rays (bremsstrahlung). Use plastic first, then metal.

γ

Gamma: no chargeA photon travels freely until a single event removes it: photoelectric absorption, Compton scattering or pair production. Each layer removes the same fraction, I = I0e−μx, so there is no range. Dense, high-Z materials make μ large.

n

Neutron: no charge, sees only nucleiA neutron ignores the electrons and travels until it hits a nucleus. It mostly bounces off (elastic scattering), and it loses the most energy when the nucleus has about the same mass as it does. A proton has nearly the same mass, so hydrogen-rich water, concrete and polyethylene slow it down in about 20 collisions. A lead nucleus is 200 times heavier and the neutron bounces off it with almost no energy loss. Once slow (thermal), the neutron is captured, and the capture releases gamma rays, so a neutron shield needs a gamma shield too.

Nuclear fission · the chain reaction

Run a reactor

Each ²³⁵U fission releases 2 or 3 fast neutrons. Water slows them down so another ²³⁵U nucleus can capture one, and the control rods absorb the rest. Pull the rods out slowly until each fission causes exactly one more: the reactor is critical. Then bring it up to full power without tripping the safety system.

    Why a reactor can be controlled

    M

    The moderator slows neutrons downFission neutrons are born at about 2 MeV. At that energy ²³⁵U fissions with a cross section of about 1 barn, but at thermal energy (0.025 eV) it is 585 barns. About 20 collisions with the hydrogen in water bring a neutron down to thermal energy.

    B

    Control rods absorb themBoron-10 and cadmium absorb thermal neutrons thousands of times more readily than water. Inserting the rods lowers the multiplication factor k. At k = 1 each generation has exactly as many neutrons as the last.

    τ

    Delayed neutrons buy timeA neutron generation lasts only about 0.1 ms. If all neutrons were prompt, k = 1.001 would multiply the power 20 000 times in a second. But 0.65% of neutrons (β) come seconds later, from the decay of fission fragments such as ⁸⁷Br. They stretch the time scale to tens of seconds, as long as the reactivity stays below β. Above β the reactor is prompt critical.

    T

    Hot fuel pushes backAs the fuel heats up, the absorption resonances of ²³⁸U broaden (Doppler effect) and capture more neutrons. Reactivity falls by a few pcm per degree, so you must pull the rods a little further to hold full power, and the reactor partly steadies itself.
