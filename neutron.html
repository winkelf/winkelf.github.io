Nucleons in orbits

Each nucleon moves in the average potential made by all the others, much like electrons in an atom. With a strong spin–orbit force added, the energy levels bunch into shells. A nucleus with a full shell is especially stable. The nucleon counts that fill a shell are the magic numbers: 2, 8, 20, 28, 50, 82 and 126.

Where the magic numbers come from

Build up the potential in three steps, from left to right.

  • Harmonic oscillator. Levels are equally spaced, and shells close at 2, 8, 20, 40, 70, 112 and 168. Only the first three match experiment.
  • Flatter bottom. A real nucleus has roughly constant density, so its potential is flat inside and drops at the edge (Woods–Saxon). Orbits with high l spend more time where the well is deep, so they move down.
  • Spin–orbit. A strong −Vls(r) l·s term splits each level in two. The j = l + ½ partner goes down and j = l − ½ goes up, with a splitting that grows like 2l + 1.

From 1f7/2 upward, the lowered partner of the highest-l orbit (highlighted) drops into the shell below. These intruder levels move the closures to 28, 50, 82 and 126.

Evidence for the magic numbers: the energy needed to remove one nucleon drops sharply just past a magic number. Magic nuclei have unusually high first excited states. Tin (Z = 50) has ten stable isotopes, more than any other element.
